# Recursive Perspective Integration (RPI) v0.1

## Purpose

RPI is a bounded deliberation protocol for complex reasoning. It is not a cosmological claim and does not assume any metaphysical proposition.

## Core hypothesis

For sufficiently complex problems, adaptive perspective fragmentation followed by information-preserving convergence, contradiction analysis, epistemic checking, bounded consequence recursion, affected-party modeling, and corrective feedback can improve reasoning quality relative to an otherwise identical baseline, while activation and stopping rules limit unnecessary deliberation on simple tasks.

## Activation gate

Use deep RPI only when at least one of the following is materially present:

- multiple stakeholders with meaningfully different interests;
- uncertainty or incomplete evidence that affects the decision;
- plausible competing interpretations;
- important second- or higher-order consequences;
- meaningful ethical or safety consequences;
- high cost of an unexamined assumption;
- a decision whose quality depends on integrating distinct domains or perspectives.

Do not activate deep RPI merely because a task is long, emotionally charged, or difficult to phrase.

## Protocol

1. **Observe** — State the decision/problem and distinguish known facts, constraints, and unknowns.
2. **Fragment** — Generate a small set of genuinely distinct, relevant perspectives or analytical lenses.
3. **Explore** — Let each perspective identify information, risks, assumptions, and possible actions that other perspectives may miss.
4. **Novelty check** — Stop adding perspectives when additional perspectives are substantially redundant or add no decision-relevant information.
5. **Converge** — Integrate useful information without treating consensus as proof and without erasing material disagreement.
6. **Contradiction check** — Identify incompatible claims. Classify conflicts as factual, assumption-based, value-based, or caused by missing information.
7. **Epistemic check** — Separate evidence from inference, intuition, speculation, and unsupported assumption. Calibrate uncertainty.
8. **Bounded consequence recursion** — Examine important downstream effects. Stop when additional causal projection becomes more speculative than decision-useful.
9. **Affected-party check** — Model material effects on relevant people/agents without confusing understanding with agreement or truth.
10. **Act/Create** — Give a clear answer, recommendation, or next action proportionate to the evidence.
11. **Feedback** — State what new evidence or outcome would materially change the conclusion when relevant.

## Hard constraints

- Perspective count is not a quality metric.
- Majority agreement is not evidence of truth.
- Empathy must not become sycophancy.
- Perspective inclusion must not create false equivalence between well-supported and unsupported claims.
- Novelty is useful only when decision-relevant.
- Consequence recursion must terminate when uncertainty dominates expected informational value.
- Urgent tasks should prioritize safe action over exhaustive analysis.
- Simple factual tasks should remain simple.
- The final answer should not expose private chain-of-thought; it should present conclusions, key considerations, uncertainty, and concise rationale.

## Expected failure modes

- analysis paralysis;
- redundant fragmentation;
- speculative causal chains;
- false equivalence;
- sycophancy;
- excessive hedging;
- invented contradictions;
- novelty for novelty's sake;
- over-analysis of simple tasks.

RPI v0.1 should be considered unsuccessful if these costs outweigh gains on complex cases or if it degrades simple-task performance materially.
